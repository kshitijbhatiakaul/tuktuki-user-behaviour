Subscriptions, renewals and why users don't pay

Renewals and resubscriptions

  • Renewal rate is close to zero. Of — subscribers from the range’s installs whose 30 days have passed, — subscribed again — a 30-day renewal rate of —.
  • Subscribing is a day-one act. — of subscribers subscribe on the day they install, and — of the range’s subscriptions come from that day’s installs. Only — came from users installed earlier, and most of those are late first-time subscribers, not renewals.
  • Same-day repeats exist, renewals don’t. On a typical day about 1 in 9 subscriptions is a second one by the same user (topping up again), but almost nobody subscribes again on a later day.
  • Check how renewals are recorded. If plans auto-renew through the Play Store, the renewal is charged by Google without the app opening, so the in-app Purchase Count event never fires for it. If that’s the case, renewals are happening but invisible here, and they need to be sent to Adjust from the server.

Who doesn’t subscribe, and why — what the data points to

  • Almost everyone. — of new users don’t subscribe within 7 days of installing.
  • Most never see the paywall. Only — of new users open the top-up screen on day 0; the rest leave first or keep unlocking with ads.
  • Of those who see it, few subscribe. — of top-up viewers subscribe — the price, plan choice or payment step is losing nearly everyone who gets there.
  • Ads are the easier way out. About — of new users watch rewarded ads on day 0, so a free unlock competes directly with paying.
  • Subscribers don’t stay. Day-7 retention is low, so even subscribers rarely come back long enough to renew.

Adjust records that a subscription happened, not why someone didn’t subscribe — the exact reasons need paywall events (see the fixes below).

Recommendations from the GA data

Force-update old builds

  • Set a minimum supported version and use Play's in-app update (immediate flow) for anything older than 1.4: those users stay installed but barely open the app and earn nothing.
  • Send a push to old-build users announcing the new app, with a free-episode bundle after updating.
  • Watch the App version table each week until old builds fall to near zero.

Build for the growing audience

  • Commission and promote more female-led dramas and romance shows; women are the fastest-growing group and watch as long as men.
  • Make creatives for 18–24s (short hooks, college and first-job settings) and split Meta ad sets by gender and age so cost per retained user can be compared.
  • Keep the 25–34 male audience in every campaign mix: it brings the most revenue per user.

Price for who pays and who doesn't

  • Offer 25–34 men longer and premium plans (weekly or monthly passes with auto-renew).
  • Give women and 18–24s a low-ticket starter pack (₹9–₹19) next to the ad-unlock option, since they grow fast but pay little.
  • Send purchase events to GA in rupees with plan type, so revenue by age, gender and state becomes reliable.

Shift geo budgets

  • Lean spend into Gujarat, Maharashtra, Rajasthan, Delhi and Punjab, where share, minutes per user and revenue are rising.
  • For Bihar, West Bengal and Assam, test regional-language creatives (Bhojpuri, Bengali, Assamese) before cutting; judge each state on day-7 retention and revenue per user, not installs.
  • Add Gujarati and Marathi dubs or subtitles to top shows to grow the western states further.

Fix the GA setup

  • Confirm what changed in September (SDK, engagement events, Google signals) so the engagement step can be trusted.
  • Turn on blended reporting identity and keep Google signals on, to shrink the unknown age and gender groups.
  • Send user properties from the app: content language chosen, plan type, signup channel.
  • Link GA4 to AdMob and turn on the BigQuery export, so ad revenue and raw events can come into this dashboard live.

What the data says

  1. The relaunch fixed engagement, not churn. Day-1 retention went from — to — and install-day time from — to — minutes — but day-30 retention barely moved (— → —). Users now like the first session; they still don't build a habit.
  2. Day 0 is still where most users are lost. Since the relaunch — of new users uninstall on the day they install. Almost all uninstalls happen on day 0; after day 1 the curve is nearly flat, so anyone kept past day 1 tends to keep the app installed but stop opening it.
  3. Depth falls off between the 1st and 5th video. Most new users start a video, but only about a third finish a 5th and slightly fewer reach a 7th on day 0. The gap between 5th and 7th is small, which says users who get past the early episodes keep going — the leak is in videos 2–5, where episode locks and ad unlocks start.
  4. Retention has been sliding since late September. Day-1 retention peaked in the last week of September and has drifted down through October as volume moved to the cheaper Meta first-video-view campaign.
  5. Reactivations come mostly from paid retargeting reach, not deliberate win-back. The biggest sources are Meta campaigns and "Expired Attributions" — lapsed users who see a new ad. There is no dedicated win-back campaign of meaningful size; ShareChat/Moj and OEM placements reactivate a lot relative to their install volume.
  6. Organic users barely engage. Organic installs spend a fraction of the time paid users do and retain at the pre-relaunch level — consistent with pre-installs, OEM placements or misattributed installs rather than genuine intent.
  7. Most new users who leave do so before the 3rd video. See the drop-off chart: about a quarter never start a video and another fifth stop after 1–2, while only a small share stop at videos 3–4. Roughly the same share watch a rewarded ad as start the 3rd video — the ad gate appears to sit right where users quit.
  8. Rewarded ads don't bring users back. At least three in four new users who watch a rewarded ad on install day don't open the app the next day.
  9. Subscribing is a one-time, day-one act. About 1 in 80 new users subscribes, nearly all on install day, at around ₹40–60 a subscription, and the 30-day renewal rate is close to zero.
  10. Crash storms happened. App exceptions ran into the thousands a day in late Nov–early Dec 2025. Exceptions are low now, but keep this chart as an early warning on new releases.

Suggestions

Plug the 2nd–5th video leak

  • Make the first 5 episodes of the opening show free for new users, with no ad gate, so they reach the point where the data shows they keep going.
  • Start the first lock at a cliffhanger after episode 5–6, and let one rewarded ad unlock 2–3 episodes on day 0.
  • Auto-play the next episode and show a "continue watching" rail first on every open.

Turn day-1 users into day-7 users

  • Morning push on day 1 with the exact next episode the user stopped at, unlocked free.
  • A daily check-in reward (free episodes or coins) for days 1–7 to build a habit.
  • Release new episodes of running shows on fixed days and tell users when the next one drops.

Run deliberate win-back

  • Build a retargeting audience of day-0 uninstallers and lapsed 7-day users and show them the show they were watching.
  • Give returning users a "welcome back" free episode bundle, and track it with its own campaign name so reactivations are measurable.
  • Scale ShareChat/Moj and OEM re-engagement, which bring back many users for their size.

Buy for depth, not first view

  • Move Meta budget from first-video-view to 3- and 5-video optimisation; judge campaigns on cost per day-7 retained user.
  • Add the 7th-video event as an optimisation goal once it has a few weeks of volume.
  • Audit Organic for pre-installs and attribution leakage.

Fix the paywall and repeat buying

  • Show the top-up screen at the first lock with a ₹9–₹19 starter pack alongside the ad option, so paying is as easy as watching an ad.
  • Cap free ad unlocks per day after day 0, so heavy viewers eventually meet a paid option.
  • Offer a weekly pass with auto-renew (Play Billing subscription) instead of one-off packs, then turn on Adjust subscription tracking to measure renewals.
  • Remind subscribers before their plan ends, with a discounted renewal, and send a "your plan has ended" push the day after.

Close the tracking gaps

  • Send paywall events to Adjust: paywall viewed, plan selected, payment started, payment failed (with reason), payment cancelled — this is how we'll learn why users don't pay.
  • If plans auto-renew, send renewals and cancellations to Adjust from the server (Adjust subscription API or Play Real-time Developer Notifications) — the in-app Purchase Count event can’t see them.
  • Send an "episode locked" event with the episode number, so drop-off by video can be tied to locks.
  • Add a watch-time event (seconds watched) and an episode-locked event (with what was offered: ad, top-up), so we can see exactly where users quit.
  • Check video view events: they track screen views almost one-for-one, which suggests they fire on screens, not plays.
  • Turn on time spent for all active users, not just new installs.
